## Interpretation and limitations

- CIDEr-D, SPICE, SPIDEr and SPIDEr-FL depend on the reference set (CIDEr-D's document frequencies come from the references of each call), so five-reference (E12) and four-reference (E13/E14) scores are not directly comparable.
- BLEU and METEOR corpus values are computed from corpus statistics, not as the mean of per-item scores; bootstrap intervals are on the per-item mean and are reported next to the corpus value.
- BERTScore follows aac-metrics: roberta-large's last layer (24), no IDF, the maximum over references. Raw F1 is compressed near 1; the rescaled F1 (layer-24 baseline) is reported as a secondary column.
- FENSE, SPIDEr-FL and MACE multiply a caption's score by a fixed factor when the fluency-error detector flags it (probability above 0.9, or 0.97 for MACE). The detector also flags some short, plain captions, and its input is cut at 64 subwords.
- Malformed (JSON-like) captions are scored as returned after PTB tokenization: braces and quotes are removed but key names remain, which lowers lexical and semantic scores.
- MS-CLAP 2023 sees 7 s of audio at a time; clips are embedded whole as a duration-weighted mean of 7 s chunks, following the MACE paper. aac-metrics' own MACE instead uses one random 7 s crop, is text-only by default and returns the corpus mean as every per-item score.
- SPICE runs without its parse cache (the cache's native library is x86-only); this changes speed, not scores.
- Every metric runs on CPU in float32/float64; scores are reproducible on the same platform and versions but may differ in the last digits on other hardware.
